01 · Market

Demand is real and growing, but it is uneven across states and segments

India registered 2.66 million EVs in FY26, and two- and three-wheelers made up almost 87% of them. Adoption is concentrated in a few states, charging coverage is thin and policy is shifting from subsidies to mandates. That mix favours a challenger that targets high-use buyers one city at a time.

What the numbers say

EV registrations have risen every year since FY21 and passed 2.6 million in FY26. Electric two-wheelers sold 1,99,495 units in March 2026 alone and now account for two thirds of all EV sales. Against that, only 7.66% of vehicles sold in 2024 were electric, well short of the 30% target for 2030. NITI Aayog also notes that 75% of India's vehicles are two-wheelers, so the scooter segment is where the transition will be won or lost.

Where demand sits

Penetration is very uneven. In calendar 2025 Tripura led at 18.38%, followed by Assam, Delhi, Kerala and Goa. Karnataka holds about 21% of public chargers, and BBC reports that four of India's 28 states hold more than half of them. For a scooter brand this makes the home-charging habit more important than any public network: NITI Aayog points out that two-wheelers are mostly charged at home on residential connections.

Tailwinds

GST on EVs was cut from 12% to 5%, and on chargers from 18% to 5%. Fuel is a second push. India imports about 90% of its oil, and state fuel retailers raised pump prices in 2026 after four steady years. Tighter CAFE-3 norms and a draft Delhi policy to stop registering new petrol two- and three-wheelers by 2027 point the same way.

Headwinds that shape our plan

Range anxiety is still the main worry for buyers, battery supply depends on China (70% to 80% of lithium and cobalt refining, nearly 90% of rare-earth separation), and NITI Aayog finds that many buyers see the sticker price but not the running-cost saving. We answer these with RealRange, localised sourcing and a total-cost message.

The supply side is investing. Battery and EV powertrain components are growing about 13% a year while ICE powertrain parts decline, and India's auto-component sector (about $80 billion) targets $200 billion by 2030.

So what

The headroom is large where we play, but buyers need proof on range, running cost and service. That is why the plan enters city by city and leads with those three.

How the score works

Each city gets a 1 to 5 rating on five factors. The default weights are market size 25%, customer fit 25%, EV readiness and policy 20%, competitive whitespace 20% and service feasibility 10%. The weighted sum is scaled to 100. Population alone never decides the answer.

Why Tier-2 cities go first

SwadesiGo's early wins came from affordable pricing and a durable design in smaller cities. Jaipur, Indore, Coimbatore, Lucknow and Bhubaneswar combine solid two-wheeler demand with fewer entrenched rivals, so the Day-100 gate can be passed on a small budget.

Why the metros come late

Bengaluru has the biggest market rating (5) and the lowest whitespace rating (1). Pune scores well on market size but is crowded, so it sits in Wave 2 even though it ranks above some Wave 1 candidates on raw market. Metros enter through fleet tie-ups rather than mass media, to avoid a high-CAC price war with Ola, Ather and TVS.

Clusters, not scattered pins

Spares, technicians and charging partners are shared inside a region. North: Jaipur, Lucknow. West-Central: Indore, Ahmedabad, Pune. South: Coimbatore, Kochi, Hyderabad, Bengaluru. East: Bhubaneswar. Each Wave 2 city is unlocked by a nearby Wave 1 city that has cleared its gate.

03 · Customers and brand

Sell to the job the scooter does, and lead with the rupees saved

Three buyers drive the plan: delivery partners, college students and small-business owners. Each needs something different, so each gets a variant, a financing route and a message. The strongest message for all three is running cost.

The Hustler: delivery and gig riders

The scooter is their income, so uptime and range matter most, followed by running cost. They ride 2,000 to 3,000 km a month, which makes them the biggest winners from electric running costs. They get the Pro-Fleet variant and a lease repaid from payouts. Lead cities: Jaipur, Hyderabad, Lucknow.

The Explorer: students and early-career riders

They want a price they can afford, a low EMI, good looks and an app. They ride less, so the saving is smaller, which is why the Base variant and an easy EMI matter. Lead cities: Indore, Bhubaneswar, Pune.

The Builder: small businesses and kiranas

They carry loads over broken roads and cannot afford breakdowns. Payload, durability and service coverage decide the sale. They get the Cargo variant. Lead cities: Coimbatore, Ahmedabad, Lucknow.

The brand story

Three pillars carry the national message: built for India (roads, weather, budgets), built for your pocket (rupees per kilometre, not horsepower) and built for tomorrow (local jobs and lower emissions). "Made in India" is told through local jobs and economic gain, not slogans.

Campaigns for Tier-2 and Tier-3

Kitna Bachaya? puts the fuel-versus-electricity saving on a rupee board at mandis and tech parks. NITI Aayog finds that buyers often miss the running-cost saving, so this closes a known gap. India Ki Sadkon Ka Test shows unedited stress tests on rough roads with real loads. Apna EV, Apni Tarakki uses local Champions who share real earnings and travel stories.

Price sensitivity and positioning

Buyers in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities compare price, running cost and trust at once. The top four brands (TVS, Bajaj, Ather and Vida) now hold about 76% of e-2W sales, and TVS funds its EV pricing from petrol-vehicle cash flow, so it can match any cut. We do not chase the lowest sticker. We compete on fit for the job and keep the discount cap at 8%.

Why we do not discount

Pure-play EV makers earn thin margins even at scale. Ola's FY25 gross margin was 17.9% and Ather's FY26 gross margin was 21.1%, according to the team's pricing note. Our plan starts at 16.7% in year 1 and reaches 21.3% in year 2, so discounting would erase the model. Festive offers use exchange bonuses and accessory bundles, never permanent cuts.

Financing is part of the product

NBFCs fund Base buyers, lease-to-own serves Cargo, and Pro-Fleet repayment is deducted from rider payouts through a delivery-platform partner. SwadesiGo does not become a lender. A battery subscription, which would cut the upfront price by roughly 30%, is held for phase 2 so launch does not depend on it. NITI Aayog lists the cost of financing vehicles as a main barrier, which is why this sits at the centre of the offer.

No subsidy assumed

Central incentives are time-limited, so every variant is priced to work at zero subsidy. Any subsidy that remains is upside.

05 · SwadesiGo Circle

The 100 Champions playbook: build demand before a dealership

Instead of opening large showrooms, SwadesiGo starts every city with 100 local early adopters: delivery riders, students and small-business owners. They become Champions who give real test rides and refer people they know. Showrooms come later, and only where demand has already shown up.

Champions per city1001,000 across ten cities by month 24
Test rides by day 100800Eight per Champion, about 2.4 a month
CAC gate₹9,000Per scooter delivered, at day 100
Franchise trigger60+Sales a month, two months running

What the Circle is

The SwadesiGo Circle is a "demand before dealership" model. A traditional launch rents a showroom, buys stock and hopes customers come. The Circle reverses the order: recruit people who already ride every day, put the scooter under them, and let the city show whether it wants more.

What a Champion does

A Champion is a trusted local rider or owner, not a paid influencer. Champions give friends, colleagues and neighbours a real test ride, and refer people who are ready to buy. Together they work as a decentralised sales force that produces organic test rides and referrals without a showroom or a large advertising budget.

Why it lowers CAC

A recommendation from a rider you know is more trusted than an advert, and it costs far less than paid media. Our design target is a CAC that falls from about ₹10,000 in the first quarter to ₹6,000 by the eighth as referrals replace paid acquisition. That is a target to be proven by the day-100 gate in each city, not a measured result.

The day-100 gate

Every city gets 100 days. To pass, it needs 100 Champions, 800 test rides and a CAC below ₹9,000. A city that passes unlocks a pop-up hub. A city that falls short gets one extra experiment or is paused, so money follows proof.

Three stages, each unlocked by the last

Stage 1 is digital outreach plus Champions with almost no capex. Stage 2 is a container pop-up hub at a fuel pump or mandi, about ₹3.5 lakh. Stage 3 is a franchise sales and service hub funded by a partner, opened only after 60 or more sales a month for two months running.

06 · RealRange™ telematics engine

A range number a rider can trust, calculated for their own road

Other brands print a lab (ARAI) range. RealRange is an AI-driven feature in the rider's app that calculates the dynamic range from the weight on the scooter, the city's temperature, traffic and elevation. In Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities, where public charging is thin, that confidence builds trust and removes range anxiety.

Range now79 km93% of the rated 85 km

Live inputs6Load, temperature, traffic, elevation, battery health, riding style
Scooters feeding data28,000Cumulative by month 24

RealRange estimator

Illustrative coefficients. Real values are fitted from fleet telemetry.

The problem it solves

Lab range is measured under ideal conditions. A delivery rider carrying a bag in 41 °C heat through stop-and-go traffic gets far less. Nomura names range anxiety as a key deterrent for Indian EV buyers, and public charging is uneven, with a few states holding most chargers. A range figure that matches the rider's day is a sales tool as well as a service feature.

How it works

The scooter reports payload (through weight sensing or a rider profile), ambient temperature, speed and stop pattern, elevation change and battery health to the app. A model turns these into a live range for the current trip and a typical range for the city and use case. Over time the estimates improve as more scooters report in.

How it helps the plan

In the Circle, Champions can show a prospect the range real riders get in their own city. In service, the same data supports predictive maintenance: cell-voltage drift and temperature spikes trigger early alerts, which reduces roadside failures. For fleets, the dashboard shows uptime and location. We do not claim a saving figure yet. We will measure it in the pilot cities.

Roadmap

Phase 1 (months 1 to 6) ships RealRange. Phase 2 (months 6 to 15) adds predictive maintenance and retention tools such as service reminders and a savings tracker. Phase 3 (months 15 to 24) adds the fleet dashboard and spares planning. Throughout, enquiries from cities where we have no presence feed back into Growth OS as a signal for the next launch.

07 · Financials

Operating breakeven in the eighth quarter, on a ₹45 Cr raise with a thin buffer

The model sells 6,000 scooters in year 1 and 22,000 in year 2 at a net price of ₹80,000 after GST and channel margin. Gross margin rises from 14% to 22.5% and CAC falls from ₹10,000 to ₹6,000. Change any assumption below and the breakeven and funding need update.

Assumptions

Base case sits at the centre of every slider.

What drives the result

Volume grows 3.7 times from year 1 to year 2 as each city that clears its day-100 gate adds sales. Gross margin climbs through local sourcing and cell volume. CAC falls as referrals replace paid media. Fixed costs (team, R&D, network) rise more slowly than revenue, which is what finally turns EBITDA positive.

Funding need

The raise has to cover three things: the peak cumulative EBITDA loss, about ₹12 Cr of capex (tooling, pop-up hubs, spares hubs) and working capital of about 20 days of revenue. Together that is close to the ₹45 Cr raise. The buffer is thin, so NBFC-funded retail finance and franchise-funded stock are essential, not optional.

How it can go wrong

A 10% volume miss still leaves the eighth quarter marginally positive. A 20% miss pushes breakeven past month 24 unless fixed costs are cut. Battery cell prices, the end of central subsidies and a price response from TVS or Bajaj are the main outside risks. The model assumes no subsidy and no price war, so use the sliders to test both.

What this is and is not

These are team-model figures built from the case brief, not company data. Year 1 gross margin (16.7%) sits below the 20% launch target for pricing, which is a gap to close with the supply chain before submission.

How to read the tags

Sourced figures come from the government report, trade press or consultancy pages listed above. Case items come from the competition brief. Assumption items are the team's own inputs, and every one can be changed. Verify items came from working notes and should be re-checked against the original before you quote them to judges.

Known differences between sources

IBEF counts 29,151 public charging stations at December 2025, while BBC cites a figure above 10,000. The definitions probably differ, so quote one with its source. IBEF's EV penetration for FY26 (8.62%) and NITI Aayog's for 2024 (7.66%) refer to different years. State rankings differ by period too: IBEF's CY2025 list is led by Tripura, while the team notes use April to October FY26 for the larger states.

Not used on purpose

Earlier drafts of this site carried a market size in rupees, a 70% CAC reduction, a 28% gross margin and a LTV figure. None had a source or reconciled with the financial model, so they were removed. Add them back only with a reference.
